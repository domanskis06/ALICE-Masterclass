Build a primary track filter

What is DCA?

DCA means distance of closest approach: how near a track comes to the primary vertex (the collision point near the centre of the detector).

Particles born in the collision itself (primaries) point back to that vertex, so their DCA is small. Tracks from decays, material hits or pile-up (secondaries) usually miss it, so their DCA is larger.

We split DCA into two parts because the beam runs along z, so “across” and “along” the beam are different directions:

  • DCAxy — miss distance in the plane perpendicular to the beam (the face-on view of the collision).
  • DCAz — miss distance along the beam. Extra collisions (pile-up) are often shifted in z, so this cut helps reject them.

The detector also measures these two directions with different precision, so analyses use separate cuts on |DCAxy| and |DCAz| instead of one single number.
