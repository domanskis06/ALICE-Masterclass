Build a primary track filter

What is DCA?

DCA is short for distance of closest approach: how close a track passes to the primary vertex, where the collision happened. A primary track points straight back there, so its DCA is tiny; a secondary (a decay, a material hit, pile-up) usually misses by more, so its DCA is larger. That gap is what tells them apart.

DCA splits into two parts because the beam runs along z:

  • DCAxy: how far off in the plane perpendicular to the beam, the face-on view of the collision.
  • DCAz: how far off along the beam itself. Pile-up is often shifted in z, so this cut is what catches it.

The detector measures the two directions with different precision, so physicists cut on |DCAxy| and |DCAz| separately, never merged into one number.

Your task

Build the same selection physicists use: keep a track only when it is charged&ngsp;and |DCAxy| is under the primary DCAxy cut and |DCAz| is under the primary DCAz cut. Use the named cut blocks for the thresholds, not numbers you make up yourself.
